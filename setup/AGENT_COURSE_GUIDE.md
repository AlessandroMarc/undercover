# Course guide for the coding agent

What each day of the course covers, so you can answer the student's questions about it: what the slides teach, what the labs and the product build are for, and what to watch out for. The student can read it too.

`AGENTS.md` covers the build process, the labs and the branches. `setup/AGENT_GIT_GUIDE.md` covers pull requests and pulling the latest code.

## About the course

- "AI FDE: The Forward Deployed Engineer & Agentic AI Architect" is Ed Donner's course on Udemy. It runs for 15 days over three weeks: Become an AI Engineer, Become an AI Architect and Become an AI FDE. UnderCover is the project throughout, with three milestones: a working pilot at the end of Week 1, an agent that is evaluated, durable and improves itself at the end of Week 2, and production on AWS with a handover to the customer at the end of Week 3.
- Each day has a lecture with slides, then labs in notebooks, and on most days a build where the coding agent adds to UnderCover from a brief. Ed recorded the labs, and some notebooks have been updated since, so where a video and a notebook differ, follow the notebook.
- The slides for every day are in Ed's [Google Drive folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1NQAs5rZOcgYquF74Bvt3HlpjheCSjJ6q?usp=sharing). On Udemy there's also a quiz after Week 1 Days 2 and 4, Week 2 Day 4 and Week 3 Day 4, a one-page summary sheet at the end of each week, and a Role Play after Week 3 Day 1's theory.
- Each day below lists corrections to what Ed says in the videos, taken from the edits in the course resources.
- Many models and products in the course are newer than your training data, such as GPT-6 Luna, GPT-6.1 Sol, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Claude Opus 5.5 and Jev from TypeSafe AI. A name that looks wrong is probably right, so check the notebook before you correct it.

## Facts that run through the course

- **UnderCover** is a fictional US travel insurer. Its handlers decide each claim against the policy wording under the Economy, Premium and First plans: approve with a payout, decline, or refer. The handlers are Dana Whitfield, the senior claims handler, Marcus Oyelaran and Priya Raman. Amounts are in US dollars, with a "deductible".
- **The claims:** CLM-1001 to 1025 are the queue and the evaluation set, CLM-1101 to 1125 the held-out set, CLM-1201 to 1225 the harder claims (Week 2 Days 1 and 4), CLM-1301 to 1325 the even harder claims (Week 2 Day 2), CLM-1901 the prompt injection (in the queue from Week 3 Day 2, making 26) and CLM-9001 the claim in Week 3 Day 4's first lab. Each set marks five claims as `tiny`. The handlers' answers are in `data/evals/`, which the claim agent must never see.
- **The scores:** accuracy needs the right decision and a payout within $1, and decision accuracy needs only the decision. The cost of mistakes is a business cost, separate from API cost: $1,500 for a wrongful decline, the payout for a wrongful approval, $50 for a needless referral and the difference for a wrong amount. The straight-through rate is the share of claims not referred, including approvals held for sign-off. A run of 25 claims is noisy, so one run can't show a small gain.
- **The $1,000 limit:** the agent can approve a claim over $1,000, but a handler signs off before it's paid. That's an approval held for sign-off, not a referral. How it works changes over the course:
  - Week 1 Day 3 to Week 2 Day 2: the backend refuses the agent's payment, and a handler's sign-off pays.
  - Week 2 Days 3 to 5: the claim graph pauses with `interrupt()`, and the sign-off resumes it so the agent pays.
  - Week 3 Days 2 and 3: there's no pause. A held approval ends the agent's work and the sign-off pays. The Gateway's Policy also refuses the agent's payments over $1,000.
  - From Week 3 Day 4: the pause returns, with Sign off (the app pays) or Send back with a note (the agent decides again).
- **Who can sign off:** all three handlers until Week 2 Day 5's build. From `week2day5-end`, only Dana can sign off over the limit, run improvement rounds or change settings.
- **The model in Ed's build:** GPT-6 Luna at `low` reasoning on Week 1 Day 3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at `low` from Week 1 Day 5 to Week 2 Day 3, and Luna at `low` from Week 2 Day 4. From Week 2 Day 5 the configuration starts on Luna at `none` and the improver can change it. `MODEL_NAME` in `.env` overrides the model, for example with `openrouter/free`.
- **Running the queue again:** a decided claim is final, so a rerun needs a reseed. That's `uv run -m backend.seed` until Week 2 Day 3, then `npm run seed`, which resets the queue in Supabase, along with the scoreboard and the configuration versions once they exist.
- **ADLC has two meanings,** numbered on the slides. The first is building agents with code, the agent development lifecycle: most of the course, and Week 3 Day 1. The second is building code with agents, which is Week 1 Day 3.
- **The agents:** keep yourself, the student's coding agent, apart from the product's agents: the UnderCover Agent that decides claims (the claim agent), the improver (Week 2 Day 5) and the UnderCover Copilot (Week 3 Day 5).

## Week 1: Become an AI Engineer

The week's learning objectives:
- Identify the skills of an AI Architect and Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) with the roadmap to get there.
- Accelerate delivery with Claude Code and the Agentic Development Lifecycle (ADLC) while staying accountable.

### Week 1 Day 1: Path to FDE

- **Slides:** what an FDE is. The term came from Palantir around 2007, and OpenAI launched a company to embed FDEs in 2026. The narrow meaning is a senior applied AI engineer embedded with a client; the broad one is an applied AI engineer solving commercial problems.
- **Slides:** three disciplines to learn (AI Engineering, AI Architecture and Forward Deployed Engineering) and two kinds of experience (solving real business problems and delivering to customers), from Ed's review of 316 job ads from 184 employers. Then the course map, who the course is for, the installs and API costs: about $10 typical, $0 on the free route, $20 or more for the full experience.
- **Lab:** the Colab opener, then the same notebook locally as `setup/setup.ipynb`. It builds an agent from first principles with plain OpenAI SDK calls to `openrouter/free`: one tool, `read_web_page`, in a loop of up to 25 steps. The agent finds its way through the maze at maze.edwarddonner.com. The point is that an agent is a model, a tool and a loop.
- **Setup:** VS Code with the Python and Jupyter extensions, Git, Node and uv, then clone the repo, `uv sync`, put `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in `.env` and select the `.venv` kernel. The steps are in `setup/INSTRUCTIONS.md`.
- **Watch out for:** a NameError usually means a skipped cell, so run from the top. The other common problems are the wrong kernel and an unsaved `.env`. Free models allow 50 requests a day (1,000 once the student has bought $10 of credit), and a maze run takes 10 to 15. A free model can get lost, so run it again. The privacy switches for free models allow providers that may train on prompts, so never send them anything confidential.
- **Correction:** Ed says 48% of the job ads needed at least four of the five areas. 48% needed four and another 41% needed all five, so 89% needed at least four.

### Week 1 Day 2: Frontier LLMs & Agents

- **Slides:** comparing models on intelligence, speed and cost with Artificial Analysis and the Pareto front, and METR's time horizons. Use benchmarks for a shortlist, then test the candidates on the customer's task.
- **Slides:** the course's definition of an agent is an LLM with tools in a loop to achieve a goal, or an LLM plus a harness, the software around it. Frameworks sit on a spectrum from direct calls to the Claude Agent SDK, and the choice isn't the most important decision on a project. ## Week 2: Become an AI Architect

The week's learning objectives:
- Use evals and observability in Langfuse to drive agent architecture decisions and measure business outcomes.
- Give agents long-term memory with Agentic RAG over MCP using vector datastores.
- Deliver production-grade agent workflows in LangGraph with durable state, crash recovery and human in the loop.
- Scale and optimize with agent swarms, LLM as a judge and recursive self-improvement.

### Week 2 Day 1: Agent Architecture, Evals, Observability

- **Slides:** three traps. The "agent for X": start with a business problem you can measure, and try solving it without agents first. Human-like agents: start with one agent and add more when they measurably help. Plausible against accurate: measure with evals.
- **Slides:** five reasons to add an agent: isolate context, an independent check, a different model for a step, parallel work and ring-fenced permissions. Business logic belongs in code. Ed puts data science and system design ahead of agent architecture.
- **Slides:** three kinds of evals (direct measurement, LLM as a judge and human review), the confusion matrix with precision and recall, UnderCover's 3x3 matrix with the cost of mistakes, and Cohen's kappa. Observability with Langfuse: traces, datasets, experiments and scores.
- **Lab** (`week2day1`): the 25 harder claims, with long item lists, deductibles and payments by other insurers. Trace the Rome burglary (CLM-1203) in Langfuse, score a baseline where the model works out the payout, then give it a Python payout tool, `calculate_payout`, and compare the two experiments. Across Ed's three runs, wrong decisions or amounts fell from 36 to 8 of 75.
- **No product build:** the payout tool and Langfuse tracing reach the product on Week 2 Day 3. The student needs a free Langfuse Cloud account (no card) and its three keys in `.env`.
- **Watch out for:** the confusion matrix counts decisions only, while accuracy also checks the payout. Approving everything gets 56% of decisions right with a kappa of 0. Ed sets `N_TIMES = 3`, and the notebook has 1. The optional cleanup cell deletes every trace in the Langfuse project, so only turn it on in a project made for the course.
- **Corrections:** an approval over $1,000 is held for sign-off, not referred. Referring everything costs $950 on these claims, an average of $38, where Ed says $50. The Rome notes say the belongings were not unattended and the police were called that night. Across Ed's runs, neither version wrongly approved, but the baseline wrongly declined four times and the payout-tool version once.
- **Assignment:** try the shorter policy wording, and change the cost of a wrongful decline to see whether it changes the choice.

### Week 2 Day 2: Agentic RAG, MCP, Memory

- **Slides:** RAG (chunk, embed, search) and agentic RAG, where the agent writes its own queries and searches as often as it needs, alone or through a research subagent. Retrieval evals: precision, recall and mean reciprocal rank, and for agentic RAG, whether the needed facts came back. RAG, agentic RAG or fine-tuning, and agents that learn by updating their memory rather than their weights.
- **Slides:** MCP for your own agents: hosts, clients and servers, stdio for local servers and Streamable HTTP for remote ones, common uses and trade-offs. A tool only for your own agent usually doesn't need MCP. Short-term and long-term memory, with AgentCore Memory's four strategies as a preview of Week 3.
- **Lab** (`week2day2`): the 25 even harder claims need facts that aren't in the claim, such as when a storm was named or what a Halden Arc is. An assistant stores 33 facts through the Qdrant MCP server (`qdrant-store` and `qdrant-find`), then the claim agent searches them, read-only, as its long-term memory. Langfuse compares the agent with and without memory, with a `found_fact` score for retrieval. In Ed's run it got 14 of the 25 fully right without memory and all 25 with it.
- **No product build:** Qdrant is for this lab only. In the product the facts become reference data in Week 3 Day 3's context layer, and AgentCore Memory holds the agent's notes on each customer.
- **Watch out for:** the first connection downloads the Qdrant server and a small embedding model. Qdrant's local database allows one process at a time, so the notebook retries tool calls. Today's runs use Luna at `low`, where Day 1 used `none`, so compare today's two runs with each other. The free version stores the facts by calling `qdrant-store` directly to save requests.
- **Assignments:** compare with a plain word search over `data/knowledge.json`, give each customer a Markdown memory file and, as a stretch, move retrieval into a research subagent.

### Week 2 Day 3: Durable State & Human in the Loop

- **Slides:** durable execution, a run that can stop partway and carry on. LangGraph's state, nodes, fixed and conditional edges, reducers and super-steps, with checkpoints saved after every step on a thread, one per claim. `interrupt()` pauses for a human and `Command(resume=...)` carries on.
- **Slides:** never pay twice. After a crash the node that was running starts again, and code before `interrupt()` runs again on resume, so the payment gets a node of its own and checks the claim ID. Use `durability="sync"`. Designing the human's step: when to ask, what to show, what to record, and decision fatigue.
- **Lab** (`week2day3`): set up a free Supabase project, with the session pooler's connection string as `DATABASE_URL`, and add the read-only Supabase MCP server to the coding agent. Rebuild the claim agent as a hand-written graph (agent, tools, decide, approval and pay), pause CLM-1003 for approval and resume it, try to pay twice, then restart the kernel with CLM-1008 paused and carry on from Supabase.
- **Build** (DURABLE): the records move to Supabase, the claim agent becomes a hand-written graph with `PostgresSaver`, and a handler's sign-off on the dashboard resumes the graph so the agent pays. An interrupted run carries on its claims, and the build brings in Week 2 Day 1's payout tool and Langfuse tracing. `npm run dev` now also starts the agent's service on port 8002.
- **Watch out for:** from this build, `npm run seed` resets the queue in Supabase, and `uv run -m backend.seed` only resets the SQLite file the tests use. The database password is easiest as letters and numbers, and it replaces `[YOUR-PASSWORD]`, brackets included. A free Supabase project pauses after a week without use; restore it from the Supabase dashboard. If Supabase doesn't show in `/mcp`, restart Claude Code. Other coding agents are in Supabase's MCP guide; keep `project_ref` and `read_only=true`.
- **Watch out for:** after the kernel restart, run the cells above Part 3, then carry on after the box, without rerunning the cell that starts the tornado claim. Ed's build uses DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, where the lab uses Luna.
- **Corrections:** durable execution doesn't by itself stop a step running twice; the pay node's check does. Going back to an earlier checkpoint rewinds the graph's state but doesn't undo a payment, which would take a separate business action such as a refund.
- **Assignments:** let the handler reject a payout with a note (CLM-1011 is the suggested claim), and as a stretch, resume from a second computer, or kill the agent partway through a run and check it recovers.

### Week 2 Day 4: Agent Swarms

- **Slides:** a team is a few agents on one task (Claude Code's agent teams), a swarm is many agents in parallel (Kimi Agent Swarm), and a fleet is every agent a business runs (LangSmith Fleet). A LangGraph dispatcher with no LLM: `Send` fans out one claim per worker, `max_concurrency` caps them and a reducer collects the results. Then limits and retries: 429s with `RetryPolicy`, Supabase's 15 pooler connections, and retries that must never pay twice.
- **Lab** (`week2day4`): build the dispatcher with a one-second stand-in and no model calls, try caps of 1, 5 and 25, and retry a simulated 429. Then run Week 2 Day 1's payout agent on the 25 harder claims as a swarm of 5 and of 25, traced and scored in Langfuse. Accuracy moves a little between runs while the time falls, and with 25 at once the run takes about as long as its slowest claim.
- **Build** (SWARM, optional): the dispatcher replaces the queue agent and starts from the dashboard with N agents, 3 by default. Every claim records its model cost and active time. Evaluations run copies of the queue or held-out claims, and a Scoreboard page compares them.
- **Watch out for:** Week 2 Day 5's build starts from this one, so a student who skips it should `git checkout week2day4-end` before Day 5. Start N small, especially on free models, which OpenRouter limits to 20 requests a minute.
- **Watch out for:** in the product each claim keeps its own thread, so a claim waiting days for sign-off doesn't hold up the others; the lab's swarm is one call with no checkpointer. Ed's swarm of 25 was faster than 5 but not five times faster, and he suspects contention for Supabase's connections.
- **Assignments:** add a `score_run` node that runs after the workers, and as a stretch, give the dispatcher a checkpointer and restart the kernel partway through a dispatch.

### Week 2 Day 5: Self-Improvement, RSI

- **Slides:** LLM as a judge, its weakness (a judge is also generating plausible tokens) and how to ground it, best with an expert's reference answer. The judge is Jev from TypeSafe AI, which picks a grade (A, B, C or F) against the handler's reason instead of writing text.
- **Slides:** the improver reads the evaluation results and proposes a change to the claim agent's prompt, model or reasoning. A human approves it, and evaluations on the held-out set decide whether it's kept, using objectives, noise thresholds, tolerances and a budget. The improver's recipe ends by improving the recipe, which is the recursive part. Then Supabase Auth for handler sign-in.
- **Lab** (`week2day5`): calibrate Jev on CLM-1001, measure noise by running the same version twice on the held-out set, then run the improver, with `HumanInTheLoopMiddleware` pausing for approval. Keep-or-roll-back is decided on the held-out set, to avoid overfitting to the evaluation set. The lab ends with the product's setup: the three handlers as Supabase Auth users with sign-ups off, `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` and `HANDLER_PASSWORD` in `.env`, and a credit limit on the OpenRouter key.
- **Build** (IMPROVE): handler sign-in, configuration versions with diffs, a Settings page, Jev in evaluations, and improvement rounds on the dashboard that the senior handler approves. In Ed's build the claim agent starts on Luna at `none`, the improver uses GPT-6.1 Sol at `medium`, and its recipe tries other models and reasoning levels before adding rules to the prompt.
- **Watch out for:** after switching to a Day 5 branch, run `npm run seed` as well as `npm install`. On free models, set `MODEL_NAME` before seeding; Jev is skipped, and a round needs far more requests than free models allow in a day, so build and test but don't run rounds. The free notebook uses one claim from each set to fit the 50 free requests. A run of 25 claims isn't a ranking of models.
- **Corrections:** Ed says a grade of 1 is a B. The scale runs from 0 for F to 3 for A, so 1 is a C and 2 is a B. When the first change is rolled back he says a higher grade is worse; higher is better, and its grade rose by less than the noise threshold. Karpathy's autoresearch is a recent example of a self-improvement loop, not the first. The improver never sees the held-out claims, but their scores choose the versions, so a final check needs a separate test set.
- **Assignments** for the end of Week 2: run more rounds, and apply the week's work to a problem of your own.

## Week 3: Become an AI FDE

The week's learning objectives:
- Lead AI initiatives with your clients from discovery and metrics to governance, responsible AI and handover.
- Secure agents against prompt injection and the lethal trifecta with guardrails, policies and AI gateways.
- Create a data and integration architecture for agents with an enterprise context layer and MCP gateway.

AWS calls it Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, and the course says AWS Bedrock AgentCore for the same product. Model calls stay on OpenRouter all week.

### Week 3 Day 1: AI Solutions Architect

- **Slides:** the agent development lifecycle (the ADLC's first meaning) in five phases: discovery, R&D, development, implementation and maintenance, with ownership, governance, evals and change management throughout, and the FDE's work in each. Discovery sorts use cases into automation, acceleration and differentiation, with three questions for each.
- **Slides:** systems architecture (gateways, integrations and the context layer; start simple and expect a redesign within a year), and where an agent runs: your own infrastructure, a managed runtime like AgentCore, or the model lab's own. Governance with tollgates, autonomy as a risk decision for each action, and "treat agents like any other software asset".
- **Slides:** responsible AI's six pillars, privacy with LLM providers, accountability, handover (the client runs it without the FDE) and adoption. Udemy's Role Play with UnderCover's head of risk and compliance comes after this.
- **Lab** (`week3day1`): set up AWS for the week. An account on the Paid plan, MFA on root, us-east-1, IAM access to billing and a $10 monthly budget with alerts, the `undercover-course` policy and group with an IAM user `undercover` and its access key, the AWS CLI with `aws configure`, then checks from Python with boto3. Nothing today uses AgentCore or costs anything. The free route, `week3day1free`, sets up a Render account.
- **Watch out for:** use us-east-1 wherever the student lives; if something seems missing in the console, check the region selector first. For the budget, follow the notebook's Step 4 and exclude Credit and Refund, which the video passes over. A budget that counts credits shows zero and never alerts. AWS's screens change, so follow the notebook where they differ from the video.
- **Costs:** AWS has no hard spending cap, and the student is responsible for what they use; a waiver of accidental charges isn't guaranteed. The Render route and watching Ed are the alternatives.
- **Corrections:** IAM is Identity and Access Management.
